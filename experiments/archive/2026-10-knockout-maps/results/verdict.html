The prediction failed. Ten new highways turned up where 15 to 26 were expected. The earlier decades scatter almost exactly as random counts would (their variance is 0.97 times their mean), so a fair test is to ask how often a decade averaging 19 would produce 10 or fewer by chance. The answer is about 1.7%. Somewhere past a hundred million steps, the stream of late highways starts to thin. One decade is thin evidence for a whole tail, and 767 of these rules are still unresolved at a billion steps, so this is where the question stays open.
