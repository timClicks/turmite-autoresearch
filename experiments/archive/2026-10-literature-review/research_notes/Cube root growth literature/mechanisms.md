# Mechanisms and limits of the rotor analogy

Reviewed 2026-10-15 NZDT; repository HEAD da88e61b690507526ba4e2a2c017d9c7941beb91, dirty working tree. Literature extraction and proposed tests only; no simulation or new numerical measurement. Companion search record: ../../results/mechanisms-search.md.

## What do the rotor sources actually establish?

### Takeaway
Rotor results concern a prescribed cyclic order of absolute outgoing edges. Ant turns depend on incoming heading; colour cycling does not establish the rotor hypotheses. No reviewed source predicts the ants' rule-specific boundary-occupation ratio.

### Cited Findings
- Priezzhev, Dhar, Dhar and Krishnamurthy, *Eulerian Walkers as a Model of Self-Organized Criticality*, PRL 77, 5079–5082 (1996), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.5079; arXiv cond-mat/9611019. Full preprint inspected, especially PDF pp.1–3. Finite balanced directed graph, fixed cyclic exits: eventual Euler circuit is established. The infinite square-lattice random-arrow cube root is instead a heuristic and numerical observation (p.2, paragraph after eq.1): almost four visits per old site while the frontier advances a bounded distance, compact territory, hence radial growth costs area per unit radius. The sink-driven recurrent ensemble and Green-function identity eq.4 are different from one growing infinite trajectory. — [Original preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9611019); [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.5079).
- Kapri–Dhar, arXiv:0906.5506v2 (17 September 2009), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.80.051118. Full PDF inspected. Square lattice; independent uniform initial arrows; clockwise quarter-turn followed by arrow-directed step. Section III.C, pp.4–5, eqs.7–9: ensemble-mean local time has a linear scaling profile, justified heuristically by equal subsequent visit increments after discovery; Fig.6 compares 10^6–10^8 steps. This is not a theorem or an estimate of ant kappa. Section III.B uses up to 10^7 initial configurations for contours, III overall states 10^6 for its principal averages. Section IV changes dynamics by random rotor increments (0,90,180,270 degrees with probabilities epsilon/3,1-epsilon,epsilon/3,epsilon/3), rather than merely changing initial arrow bias. — [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5506v2); [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.5506).
- Bou-Rabee–Peres, arXiv:2608.23545v1, 24 August 2026, is a real accessible preprint. The record currently lists only v1 and no journal reference; this does not establish refereeing status. Theorem 1.1: iid uniform rotors, square lattice clockwise (also specified periodic maximum-degree-three graphs) give almost-sure recurrence, deterministic convex Hausdorff limit and positive finite deterministic range/t^(2/3). Proposition 1.2 extends sufficiently small independent perturbations, with periodic laws for shape/range. Sections 1.2, 2–3: exact circuits, monotone boundary routing and subadditive passage time, plus quantitative control of almost-live paths. Clock time is individual steps. No disk identification or ant boundary-occupation formula follows; its shape-scaling constant kappa is unrelated to our occupation kappa. — [Theorem and proof overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23545v1); [current version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23545).
- Florescu–Levine–Peres, *The range of a rotor walk*, arXiv:1408.5533, published American Mathematical Monthly 123 (2016), 627–642, DOI 10.4169/amer.math.monthly.123.7.627. Selected full-text sections checked: Theorem 1.1 gives a lower bound for every rotor initialization/mechanism on periodically embedded Eulerian graphs; it is not the matching square-lattice upper bound. Section 2, Lemma 2.4: each completed next excursion makes exactly degree(v) departures from each site in the preceding excursion and covers its neighbours. Theorem 1.2 supplies initial configurations with linear range; the comb theorem has a diamond shape. — [Original PDF, pp.2–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5533).
- Holroyd–Propp, *Rotor Walks and Markov Chains*, arXiv:0904.4507, DOI 10.1090/conm/520/10256. Selected full-text Sections 1.1–1.3 checked. Rotor stacks reproduce rational fixed transition probabilities. Theorem 4 (PDF p.6) bounds stationary-frequency discrepancy by a graph-dependent constant/t on a finite irreducible positive recurrent chain. This is neither a size-uniform bound nor a statement about an evolving territory. — [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.4507).

### Inferences
- Relative-heading ant dynamics need an explicitly constructed absolute-edge stack representation before applying any theorem above. Marginally uniform colours or a cone do not supply it.
- Manuscript introduction can retain the rotor theorem with exact initialization/rotor-order scope. Replace “understand has not yet been refereed” with “arXiv preprint (v1, 24 August 2026; no journal reference listed when checked 15 October 2026).”
- Distinguish theorem, heuristic and numerical evidence throughout. The older all-initialization range lower bound is weaker than the 2026 random-initialization limit theorem.

### Gaps
- No reviewed theorem yields boundary occupation, stationary ant kappa, ant excursion laws, or a rotor boundary-width exponent. No verification of refereeing status beyond metadata was available.
- Priezzhev's arXiv metadata/version is not pinned beyond the unversioned record accessed; main extracted argument is in the accessed preprint, not a claimed page-identical publisher version.

## Which mechanisms survive existing failures and deserve a small test?

### Takeaway
Keep two candidates, both conditional and unproved for ants. Prioritise complete fixed-area boundary/interior accounting; a direct local-time balancing test is a distinct alternative, not another local-diffusivity correlation.

### Cited Findings
- The repository's T1/T1b retraction and verdict reject ordinary duration/depth/chord and sector explanations after size controls; held-out early excursion features did not improve later kappa beyond area. The later cutoff association retains censoring and size/selection caveats; independent-area analysis does not retroactively pass its previous noise-floor gate. — [T1 record](../../../../../notes/excursion-tomography-2026-10-13.md); [independent-area record](../../../2026-10-independent-area/README.md).
- Local dwell, edge colour and crust tests already failed; W5's registered primary reliability gate failed. Corrected W5 interpretation confines its apparent gains to a few extreme rules. Prefix lookup works conditionally on stationary training, not as a physical explanation. Memory-walker pilot did not reproduce the joint late growth/kappa target. — [Rule-space record](../../../2026-10-kappa-rule-space/README.md); [walker controls](../../../../../notes/walker-comparisons-2026-10-13.md).

### Inferences
- **Candidate A: approximately balanced exploration cycles.** Motivation is the exact rotor excursion property, but ant extension is a hypothesis. Required assumptions: a detectable macroscopic return cycle, old-site local-time increments approximately equal away from a thin frontier, compact territory, nonzero bounded radial advance per cycle, and stationarity of the resulting coefficients. Consequence beyond exponent: old-site increment dispersion and directional exit imbalance decline under cycle averaging; accumulated local-time differences between established interior sites cease growing at their mean growth rate. Smallest test: on the same few stationary high/low-kappa rules and rotor control, instrument local-time increments between four origin returns (rotor definition) and a second independently prespecified area-increment clock. Record origin-return noncompletion, spatially conditioned dispersion, edge-exit counts and seed variation. Oracle-test counts first. If ants retain growing interior imbalance while rotor counts calibrate, reject this specific balancing analogy. Do not infer a failure from an arbitrary badly chosen ant return clock alone. W5 already disfavors simple short-lag bulk fingerprints, so this must measure spatial joint balancing and cycle coverage rather than repeat those features. Even a pass does not yet predict kappa: it only supplies a plausible bulk mechanism.
- **Candidate B: boundary renewal with memory-dependent long-return time mass.** Required assumptions for a predictive model: locally stable episode law in narrow fixed-area bands, explicit evolving-boundary definition, identifiable changes in tail probability mass and/or boundary residence, and enough exposure for incomplete episodes. Exact accounting itself needs none of independence, renewal or stationarity. Extra prediction: independently measured early duration-band contributions predict held-out later boundary occupancy at fixed size/geometry; specific rule mutations change those contributions by enough to explain their occupancy change. Smallest test: add censor-complete boundary/interior episode accounting to the existing matched-rule pilot at prescribed first-passage area targets, with fresh seeds, fixed compute budget, and failed attainment/escape retained. Validate total time reconstruction on known paths and rotor/random-walk controls, then freeze tail bands and prediction before a fresh confirmation. Reject the interior-tail carrier if residence alone carries differences, tail time mass adds no held-out information, or paired kappa changes occur without required mediator changes. This is only worth doing for the genuinely new censor-complete time budget: another maximum/quantile correlation repeats an already weakened argument. See the existing prospective [cutoff avenues](../../../../../notes/cutoff-research-avenues-2026-10-15.md).
- Do not promote a third generic “memory” mechanism: cookie/reinforcement controls already show memory alone is insufficient. Fixed-domain harmonic/Markov approximations can be controls, but require measured absolute transition stacks and size-dependent error control before explaining occupation.

### Gaps
- Neither candidate presently gives a parameter-free between-rule kappa prediction. Numeric thresholds/cohort/seeds must be preregistered separately; the review launches no run.
- No claim that long excursions are causal survives merely because an accounting identity reconstructs occupancy. Mutations alter walker and medium jointly, so correlated mediator/outcome changes do not establish mediation.

## What wording and comparison corrections follow?

### Takeaway
The exponent, cone and exact rate identity are logically different observations; none explains kappa. A stationary cone is compatible with multiple transport mechanisms.

### Cited Findings
- The current manuscript's occupation paragraph explicitly derives the cone by integrating uniform instantaneous occupation over an expanding disk, then reports failures for noisy rotors and other exponents. Those failures constrain the assumptions, not the algebra. — [Manuscript source](../../../../../research-outputs/cube-root-growth-paper/main.typ).
- Internal DLA's original paper is Lawler–Bramson–Griffeath (1992), DOI 10.1214/aop/1176989542. The original publisher endpoint was inaccessible as substantive text in this review; therefore the original theorem is not full-text-verified here. The comparison of particle count versus individual-step time is also described explicitly in the verified Bou-Rabee–Peres Section 1.3. — [Original publisher endpoint](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-20/issue-4/Internal-Diffusion-Limited-Aggregation/10.1214/aop/1176989542.full).

### Inferences
- Revise abstract/key result wording “cone follows from uniform mixing plus growth” to specify approximately uniform instantaneous occupation, approximately circular compact monotone growth, and the measured exponent. Growth exponent alone does not imply the cone. Equal occupancy at a fixed snapshot is not established by an accumulated local-time profile.
- Add related-work distinction: Kapri–Dhar motivate the rotor cone by local Eulerian balancing; the manuscript offers a different conditional integration argument. Matching the cone cannot choose between them.
- Retain that dA/dt=c*kappa*P/A and its exponent relation are bookkeeping. A constant boundary fraction divided by P/A is a measured regularity requiring explanation, not an external derivation.
- Do not transfer internal-DLA radius versus deposited-particle-count scaling to ant step time. Under a disk exit-time approximation, one could derive a conditional total-step law, but it is a new heuristic with a restart rule; the ant does not restart at the origin. Do not cite it as a theorem explaining ants.
- Do not say a stationary kappa must be one under uniform bulk mixing: a boundary layer occupies a vanishing fraction of area yet can sustain a distinct occupation ratio. A fixed-domain stationary approximation must define boundary reflection and heading distribution.

### Gaps
- The close citation trace found no verified argument that computes the ants' rule-specific kappa. Coverage is selective; this is not an exhaustive negative literature claim.

### Addendum: Meng–Cohen pocket mechanism, full text recovered

Added during the same review after a local publisher PDF became available. PDF skill used for read-only visual inspection; no PDF modified. Independently inspected rendered original printed p.2486 (`scratch/meng1994/page-5.png`) and its OCR, correcting OCR equations from the image. This supersedes the original IDLA/rotor-only mechanism emphasis; it does not change earlier source checks.

**Cited finding.** Meng–Cohen, PRE50, 2482–2487 (1994), DOI10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482, p.2486, paragraph beginning their growth mechanism and following unnumbered equations: boundary encounters grow pockets of mean area a, then reenter liquid. Put R~sqrt(A), boundary B~R^d (their d is alpha-prime), and model pockets with height h and width w, giving h/w~R^(d-1). Their mean encounter interval splits into tau_in P_in + tau_out P_out. The bulk-return contribution is assumed dominant; tau_out~R^2(1/R), P_out~w/h, asserted numerically confirmed, produce tau~R^(2-d). Thus dA/dt~R^(d-2), R~t^[1/(4-d)], A~t^[2/(4-d)], B~t^[d/(4-d)]. They recover their measured case with d=4/3 and write 2alpha-beta=1. This is a consistency heuristic, not a theorem. — [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482), printed p.2486; local supplied full PDF inspected visually.

**Inference: direct relation to our bookkeeping.** With a finite mean added area per encounter and B comparable to our boundary-cell count P, their argument reduces to dA/dt proportional to P/A. Setting d=1 gives R~t^(1/3), A~t^(2/3), B~t^(1/3), exactly the smooth-boundary constant-c-kappa exponent calculation. Their alpha denotes area exponent and beta boundary exponent, so 2alpha-beta=1 maps to our area exponent a and boundary time exponent a*beta_ours: a(2-beta_ours)=1. Do not identify their beta with our P~A^beta or their alpha with our bounding-box exponent.

**Inference: the missing kappa is still missing.** If a boundary encounter contains mean D boundary steps and mean interior time T, our occupation ratio is [D/(D+T)]*A/P. For T much larger than D, stationary D and T~A/P give a finite stationary kappa. This correspondence requires compatible encounter/boundary definitions, comparable B/P, controlled growth during cycles, and stable proportionality factors. A variable tail weight, D, pocket yield or geometry can change kappa without changing d. Hence Meng–Cohen give an important antecedent to the growth picture, not a rule-specific kappa calculation. The untested assumptions sit in encounter times, pocket geometry and yield; invoking the exponent relation cannot test them independently.

**Actionable correction and test priority.** Cite Meng–Cohen alongside the original cup mechanism and explicitly acknowledge the earlier boundary-return growth argument. Retain only the two shortlisted experiments above: add mean pocket yield per independently defined encounter and boundary geometry to Candidate B's fixed-area, censor-complete time budget. This makes the literature comparison sharper without adding a new broad sweep. Existing failed dwell/crust/T1/W5 tests still count as failures; a return-time identity or an extreme quantile is insufficient. The distinct new prediction is whether the bulk-return time contribution and measured encounter yield predict held-out boundary activity/growth at fixed area and shape. If pocket geometry does not predict escape probability/return-time mass beyond size, reject the pocket explanation for between-rule kappa even if its exponent algebra agrees.
