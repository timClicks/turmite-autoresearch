### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- **Mishra et al.** DOI 10.1007/s10955-016-1448-5; arXiv:1509.06481v2 (26 January 2016). Full PDF and HTML inspected. Square lattice; heading-relative quarter-turn then flip; deterministic after initialization. CR: independent orientations, right probability 1/(1+r); RP: randomized defects per block; CP: repeated block. Equations 1–2 define ensemble MSD. Section IIIA/Figs 4–5 reports MSD exponents near 2/3–1 and visited area near t^(7/8); IIIB/Fig6 gives analogous RP results. Boundary definition: visited cell with unvisited nearest neighbour (p7); scaling B against A, not interface width. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.06481v2), [version PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.06481v2).
- **Meng and Cohen (1994)**, PRE 50, 2482–2487; DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482. Publisher abstract explicitly studies growth of an expanding randomized region in a square lattice tiled with blocks of 1, 4 or 9 flipping rotators. It describes sustained observed randomization, delayed propagation and immediate propagation, plus a growth picture involving an interface exponent reported as 0.75. Abstract only: neither precise exponent definition nor growth observable/horizon/averaging/uncertainty is verified. Do not translate 0.75 into a spatial roughness exponent. ### Inferences
- Flipping rotators are exactly the two-state alternating-turn ant up to colour naming/reflection and time convention: turn by the present state then switch it. This follows directly by comparing Mishra Section II with the repository rule definition. Their CP spatially periodic initial states do not equal our uniform multi-colour initial medium, and CR/RP introduce environmental randomness. [Definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.06481v2).
- A displacement-squared exponent does not establish visited-area, bounding-box-width or territory-gyration exponents. Even interpreting the intended CP estimator as squared displacement cannot promote its finite transient exponent into an asymptotic territory theorem. Our distinction is model family, observable, calibration and systematic coverage; numerical agreement alone is not a novelty result.

- Mishra source inconsistency: v2 metadata says 16 pages, retrieved PDF has 20 and an auto-generated July 2021 title date; HTML has an August 2026 generated date. Use arXiv version submission date, not those generated dates; publisher typeset article was inaccessible. Eq5 needs publisher comparison or author clarification before reproducing the estimator.
- Random-medium area t^(7/8) is reported, not independently recalculated; no code, seeds, convergence study or fit confidence interval was verified. It must not be claimed to disprove uniform multi-colour t^(2/3).

## What do the original ant references actually support?

### Takeaway
Unboundedness, computational complexity and recurrent symmetry are separate results. None establishes the simulated non-highway cohort never forms a highway.

[Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01049035).

This append supersedes the earlier full-text access gap and abstract-only characterization of Meng–Cohen. User supplied the scanned original. All six article pages (printed2482–2487) and four appended enlarged figures were visually read; equations1a/b, TableI, TableII and the entire p2486 heuristic were checked against rendered pages. The PDF has10 pages, effectively no text layer (27 extracted characters). Original unchanged. SHA256: `4ef56e8a060a5166160f2734548141dcbbabd9fb69ef14f64dc34405c0d22426`. Copy, rendered pages and OCR are restricted to ignored `scratch/meng1994/`. Stable public citation: [Meng and Cohen, Physical Review E50(4),2482–2487(1994), DOI10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).

**Direct quantitative precedent.** Printed2483 Eq1a/b measures A(t), explicitly the number of distinct visited sites, and B(t), visited sites with at least one unvisited nearest neighbour. For t greater than approximately5000, least-squares power fits give A~t^alpha, alpha~0.75; B~t^beta, beta~0.50. They infer B~A^(beta/alpha)~A^(2/3), or spatial boundary dimension2beta/alpha~4/3. They also claim direct confirmation of that dimension. These are territory observables, unlike Mishra's displacement exponent. [Same source, p2483 Eq1a/b](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).

**Model and cohort.** Square lattice, heading-relative right/left quarter-turn followed by rotator flip; tiled identical3x3 initial blocks, rather than our uniform n-colour medium. Thirteen symmetry-distinct scatterer configurations,36 initial particle phases (nine positions times four headings);10 configurations propagate before10^9, three do not by that cap. TableI's nonpropagating patterns have L positions(1,2), (1,2,4,5), (1,4,8,9), numbering rows left-to-right1–9; other sites R. Finite nonpropagation is not an asymptotic result. Growth-fit errors, between-phase variation, exact fit upper endpoint and independent replicated sample counts are not reported. Initial medium and trajectory are deterministic; TableI averages transient lengths over36 phases, which must not be mistaken for36 independent growth replicates. [Same source, pp2483–2484 TableI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).

**Mechanism directly anticipates boundary-return reasoning.** Define R~sqrt(A), B~R^d, d=2beta/alpha~4/3. A walker reaching the boundary grows a small pocket then returns through the bulk; call mean pocket area a, mean interval between boundary collisions tau(R). Approximate pockets by rectangles of height h and width w along a smooth reference line, giving B~hR/w and h/w~R^(d-1). Decompose tau=tau_in*P_in+tau_out*P_out. Assume outside-bulk term dominates at largeR; expect tau_out~R²*(1/R)=R and P_out~w/h, stated numerically confirmed. Therefore tau~Rw/h~R^(2-d), and, with a scale-independent mean pocket gain, dA/dt~1/tau~R^(d-2). Integration gives R~t^[1/(4-d)], A~t^[2/(4-d)] and B~t^[d/(4-d)], equivalently2alpha-beta=1. At d=4/3 this reproduces3/4 and1/2. These are heuristic assumptions plus numerical consistency, not a theorem. [Same source, p2486 and Fig6](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).

**Inference for our paper.** Since B/A~R^(d-2), this is a close antecedent of our constant-boundary-occupation/constant-entry scaling argument. Taking d=1 in their relation gives A~t^(2/3). We cannot claim the broad boundary-return explanation is new. Their text does not measure kappa, establish our rule-specific stationary prefactor, or separate c and kappa; those remain empirical distinctions. Their nonuniform initial binary media differ from the uniform multicolour census, so their3/4 area exponent is neither our headline result nor a contradiction of it. The correct related-work discussion should emphasize this precedent and explain how boundary geometry changes the predicted exponent. [Antecedent equations](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).

**Transport distinction.** Their random-liquid bulk motion is tested using finite-lag displacements along long trajectories (p2484, Fig4: lag10000, total time~10^8), not displacement from the starting origin in the expanding territory. They report Gaussian displacement statistics, D~0.35, short turn correlations below~40 steps, and TableII mean circle escape time~0.75R² over R100–1000, typically5000 random initial rotor environments. TableII also reports A~t/log(t) for a separate walker on an infinite already-random medium. These instruments support an interior-diffusion premise; they do not prove global uniform occupation of the growing droplet. [Same source, pp2484–2486 Fig4/TableII](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482).
