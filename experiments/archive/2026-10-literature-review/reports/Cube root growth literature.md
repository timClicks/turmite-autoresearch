# Prior growth studies narrow ant novelty

Two-state flipping-rotator growth has a substantial prior literature, so the manuscript should **remove its broad assertion that no non-highway territory-growth measurement has been published**. The defensible contribution is a calibrated census of cyclic multicolour LR rules on a uniform medium, together with measured transport factors and held-out predictions. Mishra et al. already report subdiffusive displacement and visited-area growth, but their media, averages and observables differ from the present census. Their backward citation to Meng and Cohen identifies a directly relevant 1994 study: the user-supplied full text reports visited area ~t^0.75 and boundary size ~t^0.50 for three periodic initial media, plus a boundary-return growth argument. Rotor theorems establish a comparator under specific absolute-edge dynamics, not an explanation of the ants. The measured ant–rotor boundary gap survives the hole-rim split in the selected cohort, but neither the gap nor one trajectory slope identifies an interface universality class. This review supplies conservative revisions and two discriminating mechanism candidates, explicitly crediting the earlier boundary-access argument; **the closest growth text is now verified, while citation coverage and estimator ambiguities remain explicitly limited**.

## Prior territory studies require observable-level comparisons

**Flipping rotators implement the two-state alternating LR rule**, up to state naming/reflection and whether observation is before or after the step: the present scatterer determines a heading-relative quarter turn and then flips. This establishes a local equivalence, not equivalence of initial environments. Mishra's completely random (CR), random-periodic (RP) and completely periodic (CP) media do not supply the uniform multicolour census initial condition. Their ensemble MSD is distinct from their CP origin-time average called TSD; Eq. 5 omits the square despite its label in both retrieved PDF and HTML. Accordingly the intended squared-displacement interpretation must remain qualified, and it is not a lag-time TAMSD. The reported visited-area exponent near 7/8 in random media is a closer observable comparator than displacement squared near 2/3, but tests a different medium. No source ambiguity justifies inferring a territory-area exponent from displacement alone. ([Mishra et al., v2, §§II–III, Eqs. 1–2 and 5, Figs. 4–7](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.06481v2))

**Meng and Cohen directly measured territory growth decades earlier.** In three periodic 3×3 flipping-rotator configurations with no observed propagation through 10^9 steps, they fitted the number of distinct visited sites A~t^0.75 and boundary sites B~t^0.50 (a visited site has an unvisited nearest neighbour), with least-squares fits after approximately 5000 steps. The reported powers imply B~A^(2/3), or B~R^(4/3) if A~R². These are territory/boundary-cardinality exponents, not an interface-width roughness exponent. Their p2486 argument connects inter-boundary returns and newly opened pockets to area growth; it is a direct predecessor to boundary-access explanations, though their periodic two-state medium and measured 3/4 area exponent differ from the uniform multicolour census. The full text was supplied by the user after web access failed and was visually verified; the earlier abstract-only search record is superseded by the appended extraction. ([Meng–Cohen, pp2483–2486, Eq.1 and growth argument](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482))

Boon's exact highway speed instead concerns ordered propagation: displacement 2√2 per 104 steps gives √2/52. The transient frequency-spectrum observation in its footnote is not a territory-width exponent. ([Boon, v1, Eq.5 and footnote1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0004331))

## Boundary-return arguments motivate tests without explaining kappa

Meng–Cohen supply the closest prior growth mechanism. Assume compactness A~R², boundary cardinality B~R^d and a scale-independent mean pocket area. Their rectangular-pocket construction has B~hR/w, outside-pocket probability ~w/h and conditional bulk-return time ~R; assuming this term dominates the pocket-internal contribution gives mean boundary-collision interval τ~Rw/h~R^(2−d)~A/B. These are heuristic geometric and first-passage assumptions, supported by their numerical consistency checks rather than a general stationary-occupation theorem. Then dA/dt~R^(d−2), R~t^[1/(4−d)] and A~t^[2/(4−d)]. Their d=4/3 gives the measured 3/4 area exponent and their exponents satisfy 2α−β=1 (their α is area-time and β boundary-time). Our exact rate dA/dt=cκB/A shares this scaling when cκ is stationary; with d near1 it yields area exponent near2/3. That correspondence makes the boundary-access picture prior art, while the unproved return-time assumptions and the rule-specific coefficients remain substantive open questions. Their pocket/return heuristic is not a parameter-free prediction of the ants' κ. ([Meng–Cohen, p2486](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.50.2482))

**No reviewed source computes the ants' rule-specific boundary-occupation ratio.** A rotor stores an absolute outgoing-edge cycle; an ant's exit depends jointly on colour and arrival heading. Applying the rotor range theorem requires an actual representation satisfying its hypotheses, not uniform marginal colours or a visually similar cone. The 2026 preprint establishes an almost-sure positive finite range limit and convex limit shape under specified random rotor laws; no disk or boundary-width theorem is asserted. Its scale constant also called kappa is unrelated to the manuscript's occupation ratio. The older general lower range bound is not a matching upper bound: its scope includes initial configurations with linear range. ([Bou-Rabee–Peres, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23545v1); [Florescu–Levine–Peres, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.5533))

Kapri–Dhar motivate their cone through subsequent local-time balancing. The manuscript's integral instead assumes approximately uniform instantaneous occupancy inside a compact circular domain, coherent monotone discovery radii and a stable growth law. The integral is conditional mathematics; growth exponent alone does not imply the cone. Failure on noisy-rotor controls rejects an assumption or its empirical approximation, rather than the algebra. A measured accumulated profile does not independently verify instantaneous mixing. A thin boundary layer can sustain distinct occupation kappa while contributing negligibly to bulk mass. Finite-chain stationary discrepancy bounds depend on the graph and cannot supply a size-uniform growing-domain estimate. Internal-DLA particle-count time likewise cannot be substituted for ant individual-step time. ([Kapri–Dhar, §III.C, Eqs.7–9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5506v2); [Holroyd–Propp, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.4507); [Bou-Rabee–Peres, §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23545v1))

## Boundary gaps require clocks and spatial correlations

The nearest planar comparator uses the same visited-surface membership criterion but **not the same radial estimator**: Kapri–Dhar use radii about the origin and sqrt(mean per-cluster variance), whereas local runs use centroid radii and then summarize fitted slopes. The preprint body/caption uncertainty discrepancy must remain explicit; “about 0.13” avoids silently choosing one. The manuscript's powers 2^22–2^32 are matched step-count checkpoints, not matched sizes. Its six-rule outer-hull split preserves the finite-window width gap, but that result establishes neither a census-wide law nor exact factor two. The existing band follow-up observes a relative low-mode excess but its registered decision is mixed, including a failed high-band prediction. Normalized-power decline contains (W/R)², so it is not independent corroboration. ([Kapri–Dhar, §III.E, Eq.10 and Fig.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5506v2); [hull investigation](../../2026-10-hull-width/README.md); [radius refit](../../2026-10-w-width-radius-refit/README.md))

KPZ describes a local coarse-grained interface with smoothing, slope-dependent growth and short-range stochastic forcing. Matching one width slope along an expanding domain does not test those assumptions or distinguish persistent anisotropic shape. The cylinder rotor study is a useful comparator precisely because it varies circumference independently; its excursion-endpoint clock must not be conflated with excursion count. Dynamic scaling distinguishes global width, local width, height differences and spectrum, with a correlation-length crossover; anomalous local/global/spectral exponents are possible. ([KPZ original, Eq.1](https://courses.physics.ucsd.edu/2020/Winter/physics116/KPZ%20Model.pdf); [Papoyan et al., §IV, Eqs.4.1–4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01756v2); [Ramasco et al., Eqs.1–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0001111v1))

The smallest next boundary task is the already registered hull-checks science run, retaining its coverage, coarsening and matched-radius gates. It remains a calibration/plumbing pilot, not completed science evidence. Report absolute and normalized mode power, physical separation ℓ≈RΔθ and phase persistence. Fixed angular bands probe physical scales growing with R. The filtered mode-9–64 structure function imposes a scale and plateau; a fitted correlation length from it is descriptive until the transfer function is addressed. Subsequent unfiltered or corrected G(ℓ,t), local window widths and S(q,t), over lattice scale≪ℓ≪R, can test persistent lattice modes, local fluctuations and a justified effective clock jointly. An ant cylinder would change the model and deserves a separate design. ([Registered hull-checks status](../../2026-10-hull-checks/README.md))

## Conclusion

Submission wording can now be made defensible without waiting for a claim of exhaustive novelty: center the calibrated census and explicit observables, retain the measured controls and predictive tests, and remove a global priority assertion. The user-supplied Meng–Cohen paper closes the closest full-text access gap and establishes a direct predecessor for both territory measurement and boundary-access reasoning. The remaining documentary priority is resolving Mishra's Eq.5 against the published article or authors. One selective backward/forward round located and incorporated closer work, but cannot establish exhaustive coverage; original Family–Vicsek, internal-DLA and several historical ant texts also remain inaccessible. The closest growth source and known omission are resolved, and headline contributions have comparators. The practical first pass is useful, but a strict claim that citation tracing found no closer result is inappropriate: the first round did find materially closer Meng–Cohen work and its full text arrived late. A targeted further citing-paper round on its verified boundary mechanism remains open.

The next scientific priority is sharper transport accounting at fixed area and the existing registered hull checks, rather than greater survey volume or an interface-class label. Both should preserve failed predictions and incomplete trajectories. Neither a shared cube-root exponent, a cone, a predictive prefix nor an exact accounting identity yet selects a physical explanation for rule-specific kappa.
