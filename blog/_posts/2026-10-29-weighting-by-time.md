The excursion-duration test asks whether the long trips an ant takes away from
its territory's edge stretch like the territory's linear size L = A/P (a
sweep) or like L² (diffusive mixing). Twice, the test failed its own calibration
gate. The reflecting cup was too porous. The Eden-style walker and internal DLA
were compact, yet the Score still would not call them diffusive.
[Yesterday's post]({% post_url 2026-10-28-what-two-failed-calibrations-taught-the-instrument %})
gave the likely reason: every excursion got one vote, and most excursions are
short boundary returns that do not scale with L at all.

Revision 3 changes one thing. It weights each excursion by its duration.

## Question and prediction

The new curve is the time-weighted survival S<sub>tw</sub>(s): the share of
off-boundary time spent in excursions longer than s. Put another way, it is the
duration law of the excursion that contains a randomly chosen interior step.

The Score, the 0.75 margin, the grid, the retention rules and the
censoring check are all carried over from Revision 2. One retention rule is
added: an effective-sample-size floor, n<sub>eff</sub> ≥ 30.

The registration (commit `3d87d8b`, 2026-10-29 09:17 NZDT) was committed before
any measurement. It predicted four things:

- Synthetic laws built to be diffusive (D) are called D, and laws built to be
  sweeps (S) are called S, in at least 18 of 20 replicates, with no replicate
  getting the opposite call.
- Eden-style growth and internal DLA are called D, compact, and not
  censoring-fragile.
- Mean-field is called S.
- Nothing is run on ants today.

The registration also says why Eden is a fair D calibrator. Its interior
is exactly a simple random walk, so diffusion is a theoretical expectation for
Eden. It is not true by construction. That gap is why the synthetic gate runs first.

## Method and provenance

- **Instrument.** The probe gained a fine duration histogram with 8 sub-bins per
  octave. It is self-checked under `--check`, and a new mutant breaks the bin
  index. On the old smoke test, the output was byte-identical to the previous
  binary. 227 Rust tests passed. Commit: `a8a93b1`.
- **Synthetic gate.** Five laws × 20 replicates × 36 windows.
- **Walkers.** Fresh seeds 2026102951–54, k = 16–24, 4 seeds × 9 windows per walker.
- **Compute.** About 15 CPU-seconds in total.
- **Files.** Results are in `results/rev3_*` in the experiment folder; the
  per-file hashes are in `results/sha256sums.txt`.

## Observation

**Synthetic laws.** Score_tw made the correct call in 20 of 20 replicates for all
five laws, with no opposite calls.

The old number-weighted Score ran on the same draws. It called the
gambler's-ruin law, which is diffusive *by construction*, a sweep in all 20
replicates. It did the same for the version with a planted short head.

**Walkers.**

| walker | Score_tw on s/L | Score_tw on s/L² | call | registered expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eden-style | 0.108 | 0.025 | **D** | D: pass |
| internal DLA | 0.143 | 0.040 | **D** | D: pass |
| mean-field | 0.016 | 0.142 | **S** | S: pass |
| IDLA with restarts (diagnostic) | 0.078 | 0.040 | D | D |
| rotor (reported only) | 0.090 | 0.048 | **D** | guessed S |

None of the calls changes when the open (censored) excursion is added. Every
registered gate passed. Rerun on the new data, the number-weighted Score
reproduced Revision 2's verdicts: Eden S, IDLA inconclusive.

## Interpretation and limits

The instrument can now see diffusion in compact walkers that it could not see
before. The synthetic gate locates the earlier failure in the weighting rather
than in the walkers. That licenses an excluded pilot. It says nothing yet about
ants.

The rotor is the surprise, and it shows a limit of the test. Its curves line up
under s/L², but they end in a sharp cliff near s/L² ≈ 2⁴–2⁵. The rotor's A/P²
stays near 0.009, so A ≈ 110 L², which puts the cliff at about 0.15–0.3 of the
whole visited area. These look like sweeps of a large part of the territory, not
diffusion.

![Time-weighted survival of rotor-walker excursion durations against s/L (left) and s/L² (right), coloured by L. Under s/L² the curves line up better and end in a sharp cliff.]({{ '/assets/posts/weighting-by-time/rev3_tw_rotor.png' | relative_url }})

*Rotor walker (reported only): it is called D, but the sharp cliff at about a fifth of the area points to territory sweeps.*

In any territory whose shape is stable, A grows like L². This test therefore
separates boundary-local sweeps (τ ∝ L) from anything that scales like the area,
but it cannot tell diffusion from area-filling sweeps. If an ant is called D,
that has to be read as "time is dominated by area-scale excursions" and not as
"the ant diffuses". This reading is post hoc.

Other limits:

- IDLA covers only a 4.3-fold range of L.
- The rotor's effective sample sizes are small (median 87).
- The analysis band is finite.
- Eden's D status rests on theory, not construction.

## Next test

The next step is an **excluded ant pilot under Score_tw**, with its own
registration. It was not run today. That registration should either limit the
claim to "L versus area-scale" or add a readout that separates diffusion from
sweeps first. Two candidates:

- Duration against maximum depth from the boundary: diffusion gives τ ∼ d², a
  sweep gives τ ∼ d.
- A τ/A collapse. It should beat τ/L² for the rotor and tie for Eden.

The reserved confirmation seeds remain untouched.
