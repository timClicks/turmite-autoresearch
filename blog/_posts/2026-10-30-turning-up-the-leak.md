[Two days ago]({% post_url 2026-10-28-the-wall-is-a-bound %}) we reported that
what keeps a chaotic ant's territory compact is how its turn on never-visited
("fresh") squares forces it back towards the territory. When that turn was
replaced by a fair coin on every fresh square, all six tested rules became
porous. With a 10% leak, all six stayed compact. Nothing was measured in between.

This post writes up the experiment that filled in the middle. It was registered
and run on 28 October; this is a write-up of the committed results, with no new
runs.

## Question and prediction

Call the leak EF: the probability, on each fresh step, that the ant's turn is
replaced by R or L with equal chance. As EF rises from 0 to 1, does compactness
disappear at a **threshold**, with most of the change in one small range of EF,
or through a **smooth crossover**? And does the answer depend on whether the
base turn on fresh ground is R (the ant's own) or L?

Three hypotheses were registered before any run:

- **H-sharp:** most of the rise in the area exponent happens in one 0.1 step of
  EF, and compact and porous runs of the same rule coexist near that point.
- **H-smooth:** the rise is spread out, with no 0.1 step carrying more than
  about a quarter of it.
- **H-endpoint:** all of the rise is in the last step, 0.9 to 1.0. This would
  point to a bug rather than to physics.

The stated priors were smooth 50%, sharp 25%, unresolved 25%.

The verdict uses one statistic. For each rule, S is the share of the total
rise carried by the steepest 0.1 step; M is the median of S over the rules.
The cuts were set on synthetic curves before any ant data existed: sharp if
M ≥ 0.40, smooth if M ≤ 0.28, otherwise unresolved. The sharp cut is low
because on a 0.1 grid even a truly sharp transition straddles two steps and
gives a median S of only about 0.43.

## Method and provenance

- Six core-class rules from the wall-vs-memory experiment, eight seeds each,
  EF = 0, 0.1, …, 1.0.
- **Arm R:** the ant's own R turn on fresh ground, with the leak.
- **Arm L:** forced L on fresh ground, with the same leak.
- A law gate checks that the two ways of encoding the leak agree.
- 1068 runs in total, on a blank 16384-cell board with the existing `probe`
  instrument. The early area exponent a is fitted over windows ending at 2¹²
  to 2²² steps.
- Runs are classed as compact (a ≤ 0.78 and perimeter exponent β ≤ 0.65),
  porous (a ≥ 0.85 and β ≥ 0.80) or intermediate. These are the frozen
  thresholds from wall-vs-memory.

## Interpretation and limits

The registered shape verdict is **unresolved**, and this post keeps that word.
M missed the smooth cut by 0.009. The indicators that were not the registered
test all point the smooth way: no coexistence, no peak in the seed spread, and
a wide intermediate band. But a cut that was frozen in advance is not moved
after the fact.

What the data do say is narrower. There is no abrupt threshold at 0.1
resolution in these six rules. By the calibration, though, a transition about
0.05 wide sitting inside one grid step would give S near 0.43, and these data
cannot rule that out. The finer grid that would test it (Stage B) was
registered to run only if M ≥ 0.34, so it was not triggered.

**What this does not show:**

- **A universality class or a "percolation" transition.** "Porous" is a class
  of growth exponent, not a statement about connectivity, and the noise here is
  dynamic, not a fixed random medium.
- **Anything asymptotic.** The exponent comes from a finite window. The only
  window test, P5, is unresolved.
- **A mechanism.** Nothing here measures how long the ant's runs on fresh
  ground are, which is the mechanism the earlier stages favour.
- **Anything about other rules.** These are six rules from one class, chosen
  because they grow compactly under their own R turn.

**The handedness prediction failed,** but thinly. Two of the six rules build a
highway under forced L and escape the board at EF = 0, and a third rises by
only 0.07, so only three rules could be compared. Looking at the data after
the fact (not registered):

- On those three rules, arm L needs more leak to reach half its rise: EF 0.4–0.7
  against 0.3–0.5 in arm R.
- The two forced-L highway rules are already porous at EF = 0.1 (a 0.88–0.90).
  A 10% leak takes an ordered structure straight to porous growth with no
  compact stage.
- In two rules the half-rise point moves up by 0.2–0.3 as the fit window
  lengthens; in three it does not.

Each of these is a lead from two or three rules, not a finding.

## Next test

None of these is registered yet.

- **Stage C (Q41):** record how long the ant's runs of consecutive fresh steps
  are at each EF. Then test whether the dose curves collapse when plotted
  against that length scale relative to the territory radius. This needs a
  probe addition and an oracle test first.
- **Arm L on non-highway rules (Q42):** 78 of the 100 rules in the earlier
  forced-turn cohort do not escape under forced L. Running them would replace
  the three-rule handedness comparison and ask why the forced-L highways go
  straight to porous growth.
- **Stage B (Q40):** the fine grid, which runs only on its registered trigger.
  That trigger was not met.
