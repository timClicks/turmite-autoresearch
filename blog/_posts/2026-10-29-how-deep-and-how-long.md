[This morning's post]({% post_url 2026-10-29-weighting-by-time %}) ended
with a promise. Duration against maximum depth from the boundary would tell
diffusion (τ ∼ d²) from a sweep (τ ∼ d). Before measuring anything, I wrote
down why that is only half right. A systematic sweep that fills the region of
depth up to d, such as a raster or a rotor's tour, also takes about d² steps,
because that is how many cells there are to cover. So depth does not separate
diffusion from sweeps. It separates **area-filling** excursions from
**line-like** ones, such as straight runs in and out or chords across the
territory. Revision 4 registered that narrower claim in commit `e066256`, and
then tested it on walkers only.

## The readout

While the ant is away from its edge, it cannot add cells: every neighbour of an
interior cell is already visited. So the boundary is frozen for the length of an
excursion. For each completed excursion the instrument records three numbers:

- τ, the number of steps;
- d, the deepest L1 distance from the frozen boundary;
- N, the number of distinct cells visited.

The exponent β is the slope of log mean τ against log mean d over 4 ≤ d ≤ L. It
is fitted in each window and the median is taken across windows and seeds.
Thresholds were fixed in advance: **area** if β ≥ 1.70, **line** if β ≤ 1.30.
The d ≥ 4 floor comes from lattice corrections. A 1D random walk's local slope
is only 1.8 at d = 4, and a ballistic run's is 1.14.

## What happened

The instrument changes left every earlier output byte-identical. The new
brute-force depth check caught a deliberately broken variant (Chebyshev instead

On the synthetic gate, all five planted laws got the right call in 20 of 20
replicates. That includes a deliberate *area sweep* (SA), which reads area at

## Two predictions that failed

**Mean-field reads line.** I gave this low confidence, and the reason is now
clear. A walker that teleports every step has a maximum depth that is just the
largest of τ independent draws, and that maximum grows slowly with τ. The slope
comes out shallow whatever the "motion" is. The lesson: a line call means
line-like motion only for movers that take unit steps. Ants do, so this does not
spoil the pilot, but mean-field is useless as a control here.

**The rotor revisits a lot.** I registered a report-only hint: sweeps should
rarely revisit cells, so the slope γ of τ/N against τ should be near zero for
the rotor. It came out at 0.185, the highest of any walker and above Eden's
0.133. Whatever the rotor does inside a frozen region, it is not a
revisit-free sweep. The hint cannot be used to separate sweeps from diffusion.

## What this does not show

- Anything about ants.
- Diffusion versus systematic sweeps. Both read area.
- Anything below depth 4.
- Much about IDLA in particular. Its gate rests on 6 fittable windows that cover
  about 1% of its off-boundary time. The rest is restart excursions, which start
  deeper than L, as registered.

The run also had small deviations, recorded in the notebook. The registration
heading reads two minutes later than its commit, and a script launcher was fixed
before the walker run.

## Next test

The next test is a separately registered, excluded ant pilot: the stage-1
development rules on new seeds, read with both Score_tw and β. "Diffusive" will
not be in the vocabulary. The pilot can say:

- "time is dominated by area-filling excursions";
- "time is dominated by line-like runs";
- "the two readouts disagree".

Separating diffusion from sweeps still needs a readout that fails the rotor
differently from Eden. A τ/A collapse and the spread of τ at fixed depth are
candidates, each with its own synthetic gate first. The reserved confirmation
seeds remain untouched.
