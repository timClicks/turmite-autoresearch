# Summary of results (2026-10-13)

Compiled from `research-outputs/scholarly-articles/cube-root-growth/main.typ`, `research-outputs/scholarly-articles/cube-root-growth/numbers.json` and `notes/`. No new measurements; see the source files for commands and seeds.

## Headline
Generalised Langton's ants that don't build a highway grow as **t^(1/3) in width and t^(2/3) in area**. The census covers 3,512 canonical rules of length 3-12.

| Measure | Ants (blob rules) | Rotor walker (known: 1/3, 2/3) |
|---|---|---|
| Width exponent | 0.333 (bootstrap 95% interval 0.332-0.334) | 0.326 |
| R_g exponent | 0.336 | 0.339 |
| Area exponent | 0.679 | 0.687 |

- **Bias correction:** after subtracting the rotor's finite-window bias, the three exponents come out at 0.340, 0.330 and 0.658. They point in different directions, so the claim is consistent with 1/3 at about +/-0.01, not "equal to 1/3".
- **Rule length:** the exponent doesn't depend on rule length (Spearman rho near 0).
- **Classes:** the census is the 3,512 rules left after the recognised highway builders were removed. Of these, 304 are labelled "builders", but only 5 have a recognised highway period: 289 stay on the grid and fill it slowly (steps per box cell ~2-6, frame builders) and 10 left the grid. The other 3,208 are 2,622 blob growers and 425 log growers (width exponent around 0.10), plus 153 stalled or tethered rules that were never fitted. Of the blob rules, 103 grow faster than a cube root.
- **Noise:** most of the spread between rules is run-to-run noise. Within-rule sd is 0.016 against a between-rule true sd of 0.004 (40-rule sample). A larger 99-rule sample gives 0.018 against 0.0067 (ICC 0.12), and a sparse random background gives the same ICC (0.125). For the area exponent the ICC is 0.34-0.43, so rule identity matters more there.

## Why: the three-factor decomposition
The area growth rate is dA/dt = c * kappa * P/A, where:
- c is the chance an edge step enters new ground.
- kappa is the fraction of time on the edge, relative to a uniform spread over the territory.
- P is the edge length.

This is an identity, so matching it is a consistency check and not a test of the mechanism. The tested content is:
- **c is flat:** the drift exponent is under 0.05 for 99.7% of blob rules.
- **kappa is flat:** the same holds for 83% of blob rules, and for six rules run to 2^32 steps.
- **Edge exponent:** P ~ A^beta with beta ~ 0.52 in the census window, the same as the rotor in the same window. The rotor's local slope falls to 0.5014 +/- 0.0034 at 2^36 (6 seeds, one octave carries the decision), which favours exactly 1/2 over a plateau at 0.51. It is not proved.
- **Outliers:** rules that deviate from the cube root do so because kappa drifts. The correlation of the width exponent with kappa's drift exponent is 0.78. The log-growers have kappa near 0 and falling.
- **What sets c:** a rule-letter model explains R^2 = 0.66 on held-out rules. The first letters matter most, and R on young colours lowers c. The model mostly separates clusters.
- **Well-mixed ant:** this holds for 35 of 40 random core rules and 24 of 33 convenience-sampled ones. Rules with non-uniform interior colours are the exceptions.

## Frontier work (32 items, all done except the knockout map, which another agent owns)
- **Exact exponent (item 4, B1b):** at 2^30, 11 rules x 24 seeds: six match the rotor's finite-time value and five don't (0.660-0.696). At 2^34 with a well-posed criterion (B1b), 14 of 14 evaluable ants match the rotor, including RLL and RLR; their excess on a blank start was start-specific scatter. RLRR escapes the board, and RLRLLL and RRRLR break out late. This is "consistent with 2/3 at the +/-0.01 level, relative to the rotor", not a proof of exactly 1/3. The first decision rule (|a_inf - 2/3| < 0.005) could not be passed even by the rotor and was dropped.
- **RRLLLLLLR (A7, A7c):** the bulk converges (CV peaks at 0.48 at 2^28, plateau ~0.36, flat mean to 2^31). Rare breakouts (1 of 62 runs at 2^32, a 14x outlier) make the moments non-convergent. About 5% of random rules show rising CV, mostly rules starting RLLL.
- **Shape (item 5):** the limit shape is round (anisotropy 0.07). Visit density is a cone, 3(1-r/R), for ants and rotor alike to within a few percent. The cone follows analytically from uniform mixing plus the 2/3 exponent, so it is not independent evidence. The derivation correctly fails on a random background.
- **Edge width (items 7, A4c):** the ants' boundary-width exponent is 0.22-0.25 through 2^34, against ~0.12 for the rotor (which matches Kapri and Dhar's ~0.13). Both are flat, and the factor ~2 gap does not close. The universality class isn't decided.
- **Other settings:**
  - **Hex and triangular grids (item 24):** area exponents 0.712 and 0.676, against 0.682 on the square grid.
  - **Turmites (item 25):** 85% of 2,290 chaotic growers lie within 0.1 of 2/3, and a separate exact sqrt(t) cluster of 1,560 tables exists.
  - **Many ants (item 27):** the exponent is unchanged.
  - **Higher dimensions (items 28, D2c):** at 2^34, volume 0.769 and extent 0.237, close to 3/4 and 1/4 but approaching volume from above and extent from below. The fill fraction is still rising and there is no calibrated 3D walker, so "close to, not shown equal to".
  - **Noise (item 26, D3):** the area exponent rises smoothly with eps, from 0.70 (eps <= 0.01) to 0.76 (0.1) and 0.89 (0.5), with no sharp transition. The cube root is robust only to eps <= 0.01; the earlier "up to 0.1" was too generous.
  - **Arrow bias (C2, C2b, C2c):** the rotor with biased initial arrows keeps its width exponent (0.32-0.34) up to bias 0.25, with a larger prefactor, and is ballistic by 0.30. Kappa rises a little with bias (+9% at 0.15) and c is flat. The area exponent rises from 0.15 (0.73 at 0.15, 0.79 at 0.25).
- **Highways (items 17-20):**
  - **Unresolved rules:** of 807 rules run to 10^10, 38 (4.7%) were new highways in (1e9, 1e10]. The pre-registered ~1% was falsified. In the next decade (1e10, 1e11] a 240-rule sample gave only 3 (1.25%), a ~4x fall, so the flat-rate prediction is falsified too. The remaining 492 survivors are still being run (`experiments/archive/2026-10-h1-lacunarity-decomposition/scripts/run_1e11b.sh`) and are not in any number here. **[Corrected 2026-10-21: 22 of the 38 (1e9,1e10] and 4+ of the 16 (1e10,1e11] "highways" are board artefacts; genuine yields 1.98% and 0.68-1.64% (1.64% if the 7 pending rechecks confirm); see `experiments/archive/2026-10-late-highway-board-check/README.md`.]**
  - **Catalogue:** 96% are diagonal and the period is always even. Period is independent of n, which falsified a prediction, and period 18 is the most common at 22%.
  - **Predictors:** predicting chaos versus highway from the rule is weak (AUC 0.70).
- **Literature (item 29):** Bou-Rabee and Peres prove a convex limit shape, not a disc, and there is no proof route for reversible ants.

## Tethered ("hard") rules
These are the log-growing rules that stay near home.
- **Pattern-matched fields:** no local statistics up to 3x3 rebuild a home. What holds the ant tracks exact cells. Keeping a random 90% of cells in place holds the ant better than a field matched on 3x3 statistics. A degraded field gives a graded outcome, not an on/off one.
- **Knockout map (two rules):**
  - **Hot spots:** 3.5% of 5x5 tiles are reproducible hot spots, against 0.7% expected under the null.
  - **Size of the effect:** the worst 5x5 window removes 20-30% of the tether, and even a 3x3 window can cost 15-18%. In six further rules the worst coarse tile costs 13-27% of the tether in four, and under 1% in the two tightest tethers, which have no meaningful hot spots.
  - **Where:** the sensitive region is a disc of radius about 15-20 around the tether centre by the original measure. By the loss-weighted RMS measure (E2b) it has an intrinsic radius of ~11-12 that grows only 1.4-1.7x when the measuring radius grows 5.5x. The two figures come from different measures and shouldn't be compared directly.
  - **Return routes:** dropped. The partial correlation was 0.13 and 0.03 (E3, collinear with occupancy), and an intervention at matched occupancy (E3b) found no leg-direction effect.
  - **Contiguous patches (P4):** a contiguous patch costs ~65-70% as much per rare cell changed as scattered cells (E4). Part of the earlier "no worse than scattered" is that patches contain fewer rare cells per dose, but the loss-versus-rare-count curves are not the same.
  - **Which cells:** the most-visited cells hurt most when corrupted (A6), the reverse of a self-healing story, and rare colours double the loss at equal visit quartile. The sensitive windows become critical late, between 2^22 and 2^24 (E6). The one-sided lobe is not fixed in compass direction (E1c, Rayleigh p = 0.97) but follows the field.
- **Other findings:**
  - **Critical cells:** rare colours (visited share below 0.5/n) are present in 559 of 560 tethered rules, against 5% of random blob rules. Whether they are the critical ones is tested directly on only the two studied rules and 24 earlier ones.
  - **Home types:** 556 tethered rules share only about 28 discrete home types.
  - **Graded hardness:** hardness is graded and bimodal in the size-free measure.
  - **Corruption threshold:** the critical corrupted fraction doesn't depend on age.

## Unexplained (open questions)
- Why kappa takes its value, and why it drifts in some rules. It is rule-specific (ICC 0.58 across rules) but no rule-string feature predicts it (held-out R^2 <= 0.04), unlike c (R^2 0.66-0.78). The half-plane model was never built.
- Whether the edge exponent is exactly 1/2 for the ants (the rotor favours it; ants at 2^36 read 0.509 +- 0.003, inconclusive, W4).
- Why ant visit density equals the rotor's cone.
- Why the ants' edge is wider than the rotor's.
- Why there are only about 28 home types.
- Why rare colours are the critical cells.
- Whether RRLLLLLLR is truly non-self-averaging.

## Caveats stated in the paper
- Exponents come from 7-octave windows.
- The census has one trajectory per rule, but patched-start and sparse-background ensembles give a width-exponent ICC of 0.12 (B2, B2b), so rule identity explains little of the variance.
- Rule length is capped at 12 in the census. A random sample of lengths 14-24 gives a median blob width exponent within 0.0064 of 1/3 (drifting down slightly, -0.006 at n = 24) and a log-grower fraction that falls with length (14% at n = 12, 1% at n = 24).
- 153 rules weren't fitted. All 153 are stalled or tethered (a window with no new cells, median area 1,936 at 2^26); none left the board, even at 32768. They are not fast growers. Whether fast growers are missing for another reason is unexamined. The 10 grid-escapers among the 304 "builders" are the only candidates, 0.3% of the census.
- Highway classification is finite-time.
- The paper's data-availability, AI-disclosure and funding sections are still placeholders.
